# Product Brief

## One-liner
FDE Training Lab is a practical curriculum + CLI that helps a strong builder become an AI Forward Deployed Engineer by producing real deployment-grade artifacts instead of just consuming content.

## Why this exists
Most people hear “FDE” and think:
- elite technical role
- unclear job scope
- mysterious consulting/operator hybrid

This repo makes it concrete.

It teaches the learner to:
1. understand messy workflows as they actually happen
2. build AI systems with guardrails, traces, and integrations
3. evaluate reliability and economics
4. sell or defend the system to a stakeholder

## Core promise
If you complete the curriculum, you should have a credible FDE-style portfolio artifact, not just notes.

## FDE qualification gates
This repo now treats three questions as hard gates before someone calls a project "FDE work":
1. **Is the thing being sold actually technical and customizable?** If the product is already easy to configure or naturally sold to technical buyers, a classic FDE motion may be unnecessary.
2. **Is the buyer or deployment owner meaningfully non-technical?** FDE exists to bridge implementation gaps for outcome-oriented customers, not to relabel ordinary developer tooling.
3. **Is there a reusable platform underneath the delivery work?** If every implementation is built from scratch with no shared primitives, the motion looks more like a dev shop than forward deployed engineering.

## Target user
A technically strong operator/builder who already has momentum in:
- systems integration
- AI tooling
- automation
- productized workflows

But wants to level up in:
- audit discipline
- eval rigor
- business-case framing
- deployment trust and change management

## Learning pillars
1. Workflow Audit
2. Agent Systems
3. Evals and Reliability
4. Deployment and Integrations
5. Business Value and Client Trust
6. Capstone Packaging

## Experience loop
- study one concept
- run one hands-on lab
- create one artifact
- review with rubric
- iterate with stakeholder lens

## What “success” looks like
By the end, the learner can say:
- Here is why this was an FDE-shaped problem in the first place.
- Here is the workflow I audited.
- Here is the system I built.
- Here are the reusable primitives versus the bespoke customer-specific logic.
- Here are the failure modes and evals.
- Here is how it integrates with an existing stack.
- Here is the measurable value and rollout plan.

## Non-goals
- generic prompt engineering
- hype-first agent demos
- copying the FDE title onto work that is really standard SaaS onboarding or custom services
- model tribalism without workflow understanding
- portfolio pieces that cannot survive stakeholder questions
